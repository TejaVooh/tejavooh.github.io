For founders and CXOs.

By the time a deal goes quiet, it has left your buyer's hands. It is with the security team, the architect, procurement or legal. None of them attended the demo, none of them can say yes, and your champion is waiting on them too. That silence is a review process, not a loss of interest.

What stops it is rarely price and rarely the product. It is usually distance between what the product does and what the company says it does. Documentation, privacy pages, model cards, security answers and architecture diagrams get written by different people at different times, and almost never checked against each other, or against the running system.

Most companies lose the same deal repeatedly without recognising it is the same deal.

I read the product the way the people reviewing it will, and tell you what they are going to find before they find it.

Practice.

18 years building enterprise platforms, now applied to proving AI systems do what they claim.

AI evaluation

Does it work, measured against what, and can you show the run that proved it. Capability and safety evaluation built on your use case and your data, wired into the release so it runs again, with every number traced to the artifact behind it and the date it was last true.

AI governance

What you are required to document, and the evidence an auditor will ask for. DPDP, GDPR, ISO/IEC 42001 and EU AI Act technical documentation, with data residency and retention resolved so a regulated buyer is shown evidence rather than assurances.

AI security

What actually leaves your system, where it goes, what is exposed, and whether your documentation and your build say the same thing. I read the product the way a buyer's security team will, before they do.
